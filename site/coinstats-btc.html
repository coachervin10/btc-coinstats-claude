Phase 1 of automated live trading: every auto-trade this page places on its own paper Kalshi ledger is mirrored here as a same-dollar Coinbase position — long when betting over, a synthetic short-equivalent when betting under (a plain spot buy cannot literally short, so this approximates what a futures or margin short would have done). Nothing here touches a real account or places a real order; it is a rehearsal so you can build a track record and tune a loss cap before any real Kalshi or Coinbase API key is ever involved. The daily cap combines today's Kalshi-shadow (the paper ledger's auto-trade results) and Coinbase-shadow results; once it is crossed, both stop opening new shadow trades until you re-arm them by hand — the same behavior a real money-connected bot would need.

Waiting for CoinStats chart history…
Each indicator votes from −1 (down) to +1 (up). Readings that measure the same thing are grouped so they count once: RSI, Stochastic, Awesome and Accelerator are one momentum vote, the EMA-9/21 trend and 30-minute change are one vote, and the order book and taker flow are one vote. Bollinger, prior-candle momentum, the 1-minute EMA cross, the 2-minute change, the price against its hourly VWAP and the mark/index basis are one vote each, and the volume/flow readings (5-minute, 15-minute and hourly-signed volume, cumulative volume delta for this window, and open-interest change) are one vote together, and the composite is the average of the votes. The funding rate is its own vote, and it's a different kind of read from the rest: a crowding/reversion signal (very positive funding leans the vote down, not up, since it means longs are paying a premium to stay long) rather than a trend-following one, so it can pull against the others on purpose. RSI and the 5-minute EMA still come from CoinStats' chart (refreshed every 2 minutes). Stochastic, the Awesome and Accelerator Oscillators, Bollinger and prior-candle momentum use real 15-minute candles from Crypto.com when its signals are on, falling back to bars built by joining three of CoinStats' 5-minute points otherwise (marked "approx.", with approximate highs and lows). The 30-minute change and a 1-minute EMA cross use real Crypto.com candles at those timeframes; the 2-minute change comes from this page's own live prices; and the order book, taker flow, volume, CVD, open interest, funding rate and basis come from Crypto.com's trade, book and derivatives data when ticked. ETH/BTC relative strength (its own optional vote, off by default) is the one reading here that isn't some transform of BTC's own price or order book — it's whether ETH is outperforming or underperforming BTC over the same 30 minutes, also from Crypto.com. It can only shift the odds a little; the size of the move needed to reach your strike still does most of the work. Abnormal volume (over 3 times the norm) widens the odds instead of pointing a direction. Momentum can enter the odds two ways: as a flat nudge of up to 8 points, or as a drift, which shifts where the price is expected to end up. A drift matters most near the strike and fades for strikes far away, which is how a real drift would behave.

A hard cap, applied to every bet -- manual, auto, Kelly-sized or re-entries -- right before it is placed. If the requested size costs more than this share of your current balance, it is quietly reduced to fit (never increased). This is the single rail every sizing method has to pass through, independent of how the contract count was arrived at.
A bet's value is its contracts at the model's current odds for that side. Stop loss closes it once it is worth that much less than you paid, and take profit once it is worth that much more. Closing sells at the current price, with no fee or spread, after the move has held for 2 seconds on a fresh price. A contract cannot be worth more than 99¢, so a bet bought at 75¢ can gain about 32% at most, and a higher target will never trigger for it (the row says so).
Cancel on an open bet refunds what it cost, as if it had never been placed. That is different from closing, which sells at the current price. The Track record shows whether cancelling helped.

Trend & streak rules

▾
Trend gate — checks the closes recorded in Window history, not the oscillators, so it can catch a real multi-window move the indicators keep reading the wrong way. One thing this is not evidence for: a check across 918 recorded 15-minute windows found that one window's direction does not predict the next window's direction — continuation sat at 48.4% (95% CI 45–52%), a coin flip, even after a large prior move. This gate looks at several consecutive closes together, which is a different, untested question; it is not invalidated by that finding, but it is also not supported by it.
extra points
Same-side loss streak — stops betting one direction after it keeps losing, independent of the trend gate above.

Re-entry after a stop loss

▾
Re-entry after a stop loss — if a bet gets stopped out and the price keeps going the other way, this allows one more trade in the same window on the opposite side, instead of sitting out until the next window. This also applies to bets you place by hand, not just auto-trading. Needs the stop loss itself switched on above to ever trigger.
Also re-enter after a take profit — off by default, because betting against a trade that just won needs a stronger reason than betting against one that just lost. When on, a take-profit close can trigger the same opposite-side re-entry above, but only once the signal has not just edged back the other way -- it has to clear the usual bar PLUS this extra bonus, on top of the per-re-entry edge above. Everything else (max re-entries, minimum time left, size shrink) is shared with the stop-loss re-entry above.
It bets at most once per expiry, only after the signal has held for 3 seconds, never within 15 seconds of expiry, and only when you have set a strike for this window (typing one, or entering the BRTI at the last close), so it cannot trade on an old strike. Its size shrinks after losses: half after 3 in a row, a quarter after 5. If your balance falls 20% below where it started the day, it switches itself off, and ticking the box again starts a fresh baseline. It uses the same contract pricing and settlement as manual bets.

Scale into a winning position

▾
Adds more contracts to an OPEN bet once the model's odds for its side have moved further in its favor, at the new (now more expensive) price, blended into an average cost. This is a manual button on every open bet's row regardless of this setting -- the toggle below only controls whether the auto-trader also does it by itself. It increases risk on a position already committed, so it is capped and off by default.

Indicator calibration

▾
Checks your own trade history: for each saved indicator reading, how often you won when it agreed with the bet's side versus when it disagreed. This is informational only -- it never changes the live model's weights by itself. Needs at least 15 bets on each side of an indicator before saying anything about it. Agreeing and disagreeing bets are compared at similar price leads, because a bigger lead wins more on its own and would otherwise make an indicator look better or worse than it is.

Live fetching

▾

How it works: the price is a combination of last-trade prices on the BRTI's USD exchanges that CoinStats lists. The odds assume no drift by default (you can add your own drift view, or let momentum act as a drift) and use how far Bitcoin has typically moved in 5 minutes over the last hour and the last 24 hours (whichever is larger), plus the age of the combined price, the larger of that and the last 30 minutes' moves on this page is used, widened for the news window or abnormal volume, and fat-tailed by default; then momentum adds a bounded nudge. To see how close this page is to the BRTI, type the BRTI at expiry into a settled bet's "BRTI check" box; it re-settles that bet on the BRTI and records the difference. CoinStats may not list every BRTI exchange, and these are last-trade prices, not order books.
